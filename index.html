How to read the syllables

Every syllable is a consonant and a vowel. The consonant (p t v s m n l w) is a number from 0 to 7 and the vowel (a e i o) is a number from 0 to 3, so each trait owns one sound and changing a trait changes only that sound.

Nicknames

For anyone in a hurry, every name has a nickname that keeps all the same information in fewer syllables. Within each part of the name, syllables pair up: the second one loses its vowel, its consonant closes the first syllable, and the lost vowel survives as a tone on the vowel that's left. No·vi·wa Sa·no La·po·le·pe becomes Nòv·wa Sân Lâp·lép. A w that ends a syllable is written u, so wa·we shortens to Wáu. Full names, nicknames and mixtures of the two all read the same.

Every letter is pronounced as its IPA symbol: p t v s m n l w as in English, a e i o as in Spanish or Italian. The 🔊 buttons use your browser's built-in voices, preferring a Spanish or Italian one because they spell these sounds the same way. Browser voices can't bend pitch within a syllable, so nickname tones are only approximated, one syllable at a time.

Year and location count in reflected (Gray) order, so being born a year later, or one grid square over, changes a single sound. Family areas are about 12 miles north to south and 24 miles east to west at the equator, narrowing towards the poles (about 15 miles at London's latitude). The generation name narrows that to a square about 6 miles tall.
